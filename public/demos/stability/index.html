Leading versus lagging

OEE tells you what happened. Conditions tell you what is coming.

OEE, first-pass yield and OTIF are scorecards. They move after the loss has already happened, and they cannot say why. The conditions behind them move first. A causal map links the two, so a drift in a condition shows up as a forecast for the KPI, with time to act.

Causal view

Why OEE cannot look forward

OEE is three outcomes multiplied together. Each one is the result of conditions further back. Watching the conditions is how you see it coming.

Lagging KPIs

  • Report the loss after it lands
  • Average away the drift until it is a failure
  • Say how much, never why
  • Start a hunt for the cause after the fact
  • Get reviewed weekly or monthly

Leading conditions on a causal map

  • Move days or weeks before the KPI does
  • Name the condition, the asset and the control involved
  • Carry the cause with the signal
  • Point to the action, and check whether it held
  • Get read continuously, by the people who can act

The layers

Four things a stable operation gets right

Read from the outside in. Each layer only works if the one outside it is doing its job.

Capabilities

Who and what can hold the controls.

  • Human: people who know, decide, act and learn
  • Digital: systems that sense, give context, reason and verify
  • Asset: equipment that regulates, buffers and protects

capability → creates a control

Controls

The six ways a plant keeps a condition in range: qualify, regulate, synchronize, maintain, detect and authorize.

Each one is held by a system of record and a person who owns it.

control → keeps a condition in range

Conditions

What has to be true for the outcome to hold, each with one live measure.

Consistency across shifts and lots. Variation you can manage. Equipment that behaves. Transitions under control. Flow that is coordinated. Visibility early enough to act.

conditions in range → stability

The loop

What happens when a condition slips. Every loss runs the same loop, and the loop is where the learning is kept.

Controls

Six controls, and what closing the loop adds to each

Every plant already runs these. The systems that hold them keep the record. What most plants lack is the link between a signal, the action it led to, and whether that action held.

Losses and recovery

Every loss runs one loop

A scrapped lot, a stop, a late order and a complaint look different on the floor. They run the same five steps. The plants that improve are the ones that finish all five, every time.

  1. 1

    Detect

    Notice the condition slipping, ideally from its trend before an alarm.

  2. 2

    Contain

    Make it safe and stop the loss from spreading: hold, slow, sort.

  3. 3

    Find the cause

    Test hypotheses against the data, with evidence for and against.

  4. 4

    Update the practice

    Change the control that let it happen, with the evidence attached.

  5. 5

    Verify it held

    Check the next weeks against the condition. If it came back, reopen.

Count recovery, and it shrinks

Most plants count output and scrap. Few count the capacity spent recovering: the overtime, the expedites, the sorting, the engineering hours. Counted, it becomes the budget for prevention.

Where it fits

Keep the systems of record. Close the loop between them.

A plant already runs systems for everything a customer or a regulator asks about. Closing the loop does not replace them. It sorts what a plant runs into three groups.

Consolidate

Tools that exist only because nothing holds the loop.

  • Tier boards and the morning meeting
  • Action trackers and practice updates
  • Lessons-learned files
  • Shift turnover logs

Boundary

The system keeps the record; the loop assembles the evidence and checks the outcome.

  • SPC and quality analytics
  • Complaints, claims and CAPA
  • Maintenance requests
  • Energy and carbon reviews

Cannot displace

Systems of record and control. Read, never written.

  • Control systems and safety systems
  • MES, genealogy and the batch or device record
  • LIMS, QMS and certificates
  • ERP

Systems of record keep the record. A learning layer keeps the loop. People decide.

The model does not need a program to begin. It needs one outcome that matters this quarter and the conditions behind it.

  1. 1

    Pick the outcome

    One a plant leader would defend in a budget meeting: on time, in spec, at cost.

  2. 2

    Name its conditions

    Six to nine, each with one live measure the plant already has.

  3. 3

    Map the controls

    For each condition: which control keeps it, which system holds it, who owns it.

  4. 4

    Close the next loss

    Run all five steps on the next loss against that outcome, and count the recovery.

How causal works

Watching a number is not the same as changing it

Ice cream sales and drownings climb together every summer, and nobody bans ice cream: hot weather drives both. Plants make the same mistake with numbers that are harder to see through. A dashboard learns what happened together. A causal model learns what makes what happen, so it can answer the question a plant actually asks: if we change this, what happens?

  1. SeeWhat moved together?A dashboard, a trend line, most machine learning
  2. DoWhat happens if we change it?An intervention: set a lever and follow it through
  3. ImagineWhat would have happened if we had?A counterfactual: replay a past week with a different choice

The three rungs of the ladder of causation. Each rung answers questions the one below cannot. One sample line, rung by rung.

Where the map comes fromData alone cannot draw it: no causes in, no causes out. The people who run the process sketch what drives what, the plant's own history tests each link, and links that fail the test come out. That is also why a causal model can say why a machine will fail, not just when.
